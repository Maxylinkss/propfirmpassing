Methodology

How figures get onto this site, and what the dates promise

Short version: nothing is estimated. Every figure was read from the provider's or firm's own published documentation on a stated date, and where a figure could not be tied to a specific plan, it is not published here at all.

What a snapshot is

A snapshot is a dated record of everything this tracker checks. It is stored as a single data file, and the site is generated from those files rather than written by hand. That is what makes the history trustworthy: the change log is produced by comparing one snapshot against the previous one mechanically, not by someone remembering what moved.

Currently 1 snapshot has been recorded, most recently 26 October 2026.

Where the numbers come from

  • Passing service prices are read from the provider's own pricing page or checkout. Nothing is taken from a comparison article or a review, including ones written by us.
  • Firm rules are read from the firm's own help centre or plan documentation, and each row links to the page the figures came from.
  • Converted figures are marked as approximate. Where a provider prices in pounds, the dollar figure shown is a conversion at the rate in use when the price was read, and it will drift as the rate moves.

What a date does and does not promise

The date on a row is the date the figure was read. It is not a claim that the figure is still correct today. Promotions end, plans get replaced, and firms change thresholds without announcement. A date lets you judge how stale a figure is; it does not make it current.

Where a firm's rules could not be tied to a specific plan, no figure is published. That is deliberate. A number attributed to the wrong product is worse than no number, because it is wrong in a way you cannot see.

What this tracker does not cover

  • Funded account terms and payout conditions. These are separate documents at every firm, and passing an evaluation is not the same permission as being paid from the funded account.
  • Whether a firm permits third-party trading. That is a per-firm, per-plan question and it is the one you should check before paying anyone — including us.
  • How any of it works in practice. This site records figures; it does not explain mechanics.

Corrections

If a figure here is wrong, it is wrong in a way that matters, because people use these numbers to decide what to pay. Corrections are welcome and are applied as a new snapshot rather than a silent edit, so the change is visible in the history.
