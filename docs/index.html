Why a dated tracker instead of a review

Prices in this niche move, and rules move more. A review written once is wrong within a quarter and gives you no way to tell how wrong. A tracker does two different things: it records what a figure was on a given date, and it records the fact that it changed.

The second part is the useful one. If a firm shortens its minimum trading days, or a provider raises its fee at $200,000 while leaving the $100,000 price alone, that is a decision they made — and it is invisible in any static comparison, because a static comparison has no memory.

Every figure on this site carries the date it was read from the provider's or firm's own documentation. Nothing here is estimated, and where a firm publishes several plans under one brand and the figures could not be tied to a specific plan, no figure is shown at all.
