Why the IP matters

Two servers can be identical and still behave differently online

The hardware is rarely the problem. The address is. Every IP belongs to an autonomous system, and platforms that care about connection type classify those ranges before a single packet of your session is inspected.

Datacenter ranges are recognised

A standard VPS address belongs to a hosting provider's ASN. That is visible in the routing data alone, long before any behavioural check runs.

Residential means consumer ISP

Our addresses are allocated from consumer broadband ranges, so the connection resolves the way a home line does rather than as a rented server in a rack.

Static, not shared or rotating

You get the same address for the life of the server. No rotation pool, no other customer sharing your IP, and nothing that changes underneath an active session.

What we do not claim. A residential IP is not a way around any platform's rules, and we do not sell it as one. It is a connection that looks like what it is, a home broadband line, for people whose work genuinely runs from one. Use it in line with the terms of whatever you connect to.

Questions

Answers before you buy

What exactly is a residential IP VPS?

A virtual private server, meaning your own slice of a physical machine with full administrative access, whose public IPv4 address comes from a consumer internet provider's range rather than a hosting company's range.

The distinction is entirely about the address. The server itself behaves like any other VPS: install software, run services, reboot it, take snapshots.

Is the IP address dedicated to me, or shared?

Dedicated. Nobody else is assigned that address while your server is active, and it does not rotate. If you cancel and later return, you get a new address rather than the one you had before.

Business includes two addresses and Enterprise includes four, all yours, and they can sit in different locations.

Which operating systems can I install?

Windows Server and the common Linux distributions are available on every plan. Enterprise also accepts a custom image if you need a specific build.

Reinstalling to a different operating system is self-service and costs nothing, but it wipes the disk, so take a snapshot first if there is anything on the box worth keeping.

How long does setup take?

Typically about fifteen minutes on Starter, Pro and Business, measured from confirmed payment to credentials in your inbox. Enterprise is provisioned the same day, usually within a few hours.

Orders placed outside working hours are queued and handled at the start of the next working period, which in most cases is still the same day.

Can I upgrade or downgrade later?

Yes. Upgrades are prorated from the day you move, and the server keeps its address. Downgrades take effect at the next renewal, because the smaller plan cannot always hold the data you have already stored.

What is the refund policy?

Seven days from the first delivery, on the first server on your account. If the service does not do what this page says it does, ask within that window and the month is refunded.

It does not cover a change of mind after the seventh day, and it does not apply to renewals or to additional servers. Abuse of the network, or a suspension for prohibited use, voids it.

Do you keep logs of what I do on the server?

We keep the records needed to run the service: which account owns which server, payment records, and network flow data used to detect attacks and abuse. We do not inspect what runs inside your server, and we do not sell data.

We will disclose account information where we are legally required to. That is stated plainly rather than buried, because you should know it before you buy rather than after.

What payment methods do you accept?

Card, bank transfer and major cryptocurrencies. Quarterly billing saves 10% and annual saves 20%.

Orders are placed through the Telegram contact rather than a checkout form, so payment details are never entered on this page.

Is a residential IP allowed by the platforms I use?

That is decided by each platform's own terms, not by the address, and we cannot answer it for you. Some permit a VPS outright, some require you to declare it, and some prohibit automated access regardless of where the connection comes from.

What we sell is a server with a residential address. How you use it is your call, and we do not market it as a way to evade any rule.

What happens if the server goes down?

The target is 99.9% availability measured monthly, excluding announced maintenance. Volumetric attacks are scrubbed upstream, and hardware faults are handled by migrating the server to a healthy host.

Outages beyond the monthly target are credited against the next invoice rather than argued about case by case.
